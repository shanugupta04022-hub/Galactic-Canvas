The Galactic Canvas

An interactive storytelling journey from galaxy → solar system → Sun → planets

Stage 2 · Scene A

The Milky Way

A barred spiral galaxy containing hundreds of billions of stars, rotating silently in the Local Group.

Diameter ~100,000–120,000 ly
Estimated Stars 100–400 billion
Age ~13.6 billion years
Our Position ~26,000 ly from core
  • Classified as a barred spiral galaxy with a dense central bulge, spiral arms, halo, and dark matter.
  • Our Solar System sits in the Orion Arm, orbiting the galactic center at approximately 828,000 km/h.
  • One complete orbit around the core takes ~230 million Earth years — known as a "galactic year".
  • Anchored by Sagittarius A*, a supermassive black hole with a mass 4 million times that of our Sun.
Stage 2 · Scene B

The Solar System

Formed from the gravitational collapse of a giant molecular cloud 4.6 billion years ago.

Age ~4.6 billion years
Major Planets 8 planets (+5 dwarf)
Known Moons 300+ moons
Extent ~2 ly to Oort Cloud
  • Concentric architecture: inner rocky terrestrial worlds, asteroid belt, gas & ice giants, Kuiper Belt, and Oort Cloud.
  • All eight planets orbit in roughly the same flat plane (the ecliptic) in the same direction, bound by the Sun.
  • Over 1,000,000 asteroids larger than 1 km populate the main belt between Mars and Jupiter.
Stage 3 · Scene C

The Sun

A yellow dwarf star holding 99.86% of the Solar System's total mass, fueling planetary life through nuclear fusion.

Diameter ~1.39M km (109× Earth)
Surface Temp ~5,500°C
Core Temp ~15 million°C
Composition 73% H · 25% He
  • G-type main-sequence star converting 600 million tons of hydrogen into helium every second via core fusion.
  • Dynamic dynamo generating intense magnetic loops, sunspots, coronal mass ejections, and the solar wind.
  • Halfway through its 10-billion-year lifespan; will eventually swell into a red giant before collapsing to a white dwarf.
Detour · Deepest Zoom Level

The Earth-Moon System

A uniquely balanced double-world stabilized by tidal resonance, mutual gravity, and deep geologic history.

Moon Diameter ~3,476 km (27% Earth)
Average Distance ~384,400 km
Surface Gravity 1.62 m/s² (16.6% Earth)
Orbital Cycle 27.3d orbit / 29.5d phase
Giant Impact Formation Formed ~4.5 billion years ago when a Mars-sized protoplanet ('Theia') struck young Earth at a glancing angle; debris coalesced in orbit into the Moon.
Tidal Locking & Libration Tidally locked so the same lunar hemisphere (~59% visible accounting for orbital libration) permanently faces Earth.
Surface & Polar Water Ice Airless, cratered highlands alongside basaltic maria; surface temperatures swing from 130°C in sunlight to -180°C in shadow, harboring permanent water ice in polar craters.
Planetary Stabilization Lunar gravity stabilizes Earth's axial tilt (~23.5°), preventing chaotic climate shifts over millions of years and driving marine tides.
Secular Tidal Drift Tidal friction pushes the Moon outward from Earth by approximately 3.8 cm every year.
Human Exploration 12 humans walked on the Moon during NASA's Apollo program (1969–1972). Active international initiatives (Artemis, Chang'e, Chandrayaan) now target the resource-rich south pole.
