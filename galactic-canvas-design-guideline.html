The Galactic Canvas

A design guideline for the planet pages: a quiet star canvas, the planet held at right of centre, the reading column at left, and a miniature solar system along the bottom that shows how far the journey has come.

Applies to Stage 4 (planet pages). Stages 1–3 — Milky Way, Solar System, Sun — inherit the same canvas, type and rail.

The idea in one line

One planet, one screen, one column of text. The planet is the picture; the words sit beside it, never on top of it; the rail at the bottom answers the only navigation question a visitor has — where am I in the solar system?

Four rules that hold the whole site together

  • The canvas stays quiet. Deep space, faint stars, nothing that competes with the planet.
  • The planet owns the right side. Same position and same optical size band on every planet page, so moving between planets feels like turning your head, not reloading a page.
  • Text is a single narrow column. Hook first, numbers second, story third. Never more than one screen.
  • Progress is always visible. The rail is the only persistent chrome on the page.

Measurements

  • Canvas height: 100vh, minimum 520px. Page padding 52px / 44px desktop, 36px / 24px mobile.
  • Planet diameter: 300–360px desktop, 180–200px mobile. Centre it on the right column, aligned to the vertical middle of the canvas.
  • Text column: max 44ch for the hook and facts, 68ch absolute ceiling. Left aligned, ragged right.
  • Clear space: at least 64px between the text column and the planet's outer glow, and 96px between content and the rail.

      The orbit rail

      A miniature solar system, not a progress bar. It reads as distance travelled from the Sun.

      Anatomy

      • Sun anchor at the far left, 16px, always lit. It is the origin of the journey and stays fixed.
      • Orbit line: 1px hairline at 14% opacity, running the full width of the content area.
      • Filled segment: the line from the Sun to the current planet, drawn in the solar gradient. It is the progress.
      • Stops: eight dots, spaced by a compressed (square-root) distance scale so Mercury–Mars stay legible instead of bunching at 3% of the width.
      • Dot size follows the planet's real diameter, compressed the same way: Mercury 7px up to Jupiter 16px. Size carries information, so don't flatten it.

Transition is 350ms on a soft ease; the fill runs 500ms so the line arrives just after the dot settles.

      Type

      Two serifs, clearly different jobs. Bodoni Moda is engraved and high-contrast, like the lettering on an old star atlas; Spectral is built for reading on screen.

      Story factsSpectral15px · 300, line-height 1.6

      Sentence case everywhere, including the stop labels on the rail. Numbers always carry their unit, and units are never abbreviated past recognition — 4,879 km, not 4879.

      Motion

      • Planet rotation: one slow continuous spin, 40–60s per turn. This is the only ambient motion on the page, and it is what makes the canvas feel alive.
      • Stars: static field, drawn once. A faint twinkle on a handful of stars is allowed; a moving parallax field is not — it fights the planet.
      • Planet change: the outgoing orb fades and shrinks to 0.92, the incoming one fades up over 450ms. The rail dot grows at the same moment so the two feel like one action.
      • Text: fades in only, no slide. Staggered slide-ups on every block is the tell of a template.
      • Honour prefers-reduced-motion: drop rotation and the fill animation, keep the state change instant.

      Content pattern per planet

      The research document already has more than fits on one screen. This is the filter.

      • Hook — one sentence, the reason this planet is worth a screen. Written, not quoted from data.
      • Four stats — same four on every planet so they can be compared: distance from the Sun, diameter, day length, moons. Year length swaps in where a planet's year is the strange part (Venus, Neptune).
      • Three to five facts — one on composition, one on atmosphere or surface, one on what makes it unique, one on exploration. Twelve words each where possible.
      • Everything else from the research — mass, gravity, axial tilt, mission histories — belongs behind a "more" disclosure or on the comparison view, not on the canvas.

      Do and don't

      Do

      • Keep the planet's centre at the same coordinates on every page.
      • Let the glow bleed into the text gutter — it sells depth.
      • Give the rail room; it needs 96px of empty canvas above it.
      • Use the real size difference between dots. Jupiter should look like Jupiter.

      Don't

      • Put text over the planet, or a panel behind the text.
      • Add a second accent colour, a card, or a border-radius kit.
      • Animate the starfield, the text and the planet at once.
      • Use the rail for anything but position in the journey — no percentages, no step counter.
      • Shrink the current dot instead of growing it. The highlight is always the largest thing on the rail.

      Accessibility floor

      • Starlight on void is about 15:1 — fine. Dust grey at 12px is the weakest pairing; never go below 13px with it on a coloured background.
      • Rail stops are <button> or links with a 44px tap target, an accessible name ("Mars, planet 4 of 8"), and aria-current="page" on the active one.
      • The highlight is carried by size, ring and label — not colour alone.
      • Every orb has a text alternative; decorative starfield is aria-hidden.
